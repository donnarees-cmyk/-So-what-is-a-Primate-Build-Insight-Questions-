📘 OPEN EVIDENCE NOTEBOOK — read this if you need help
Reading strategy: Generating your own questions before reading helps you focus and gives you a purpose for reading.
Madagascar conservation: The reading warns about primates in Madagascar. Forest loss threatens their habitat. Protecting forests and making thoughtful purchasing choices are ways people might help. The teacher key suggests that reducing demand for Madagascar coffee could reduce forest loss, though real-world effects depend on farming practices.
Why primates need help: Many primate species face extinction. The author wants readers to understand the threat and care enough to help protect primates.
Growing up: Primates generally have fewer offspring than many other mammals. Their babies take longer to mature, so adults spend longer protecting and raising them, likely forming strong bonds.
Red List: The Red List is a list of critically endangered species. Critically endangered means a very serious risk of extinction.
Two primate groups: Prosimians are described as “primitive” primates; examples are lemurs, lorises, and tarsiers. Anthropoids include monkeys, gibbons, and great apes.
Primates and collarbones: Primates have clavicles (collarbones), which help them hang by their arms.
